# Worker CPU split on the Airspy HF+ — 2026-10-30

Window 2 measured the worker at 19 % of one core, about twice the estimate. This measurement
(`tools/window2/live_check.py --only-cpu`) shows where that CPU goes. The setup was the real
Airspy HF+ at 384 kS/s on 10 MHz, 30 s per configuration, with OpenWebRX stopped for the window.
Bundle: `k2-window2-20261030-190111.tar.gz`.

| Configuration | Worker main thread | Driver threads (libairspyhf/libusb) | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Device only, no DSP | 0.8 % | 2.7 % | 3.5 % |
| + spectrum (2048 FFT, 15 rows/s) | 4.0 % | 2.7 % | 6.7 % |
| + spectrum + 2 channels (AM, USB) | 16.6 % | 2.8 % | **19.4 %** |

**Reading:**

- **Driver threads:** the driver and USB threads cost 2.7 %, the same as the bare probe in
  Window 1b. The ring buffer and its wake-up signal add under 1 %.
- **Spectrum:** about 3 %.
- **Channels:** about **6.3 % each** at 384 kS/s. Each channel mixes and resamples the full
  384 kS/s stream on its own. That is the expected structure, not a leak or a busy loop.
- **Why "twice the estimate":** the estimate came from `make dsp-bench` on the cloud CI
  machine, which gives 3.1–3.5 % per channel at 384 kS/s. The Optiplex's CPU appears to be about
  half as fast per core. A `make dsp-bench` on the Optiplex would confirm that factor; it needs
  no hardware window.
- **What it means:** at 384 kS/s, one Optiplex core carries the device, the spectrum and about
  12 channels. That is ample for M1. If many simultaneous listeners or decoders need more, the
  known remedy is a shared first decimation stage (or a polyphase channelizer) in front of the
  channels, instead of every channel working at the full rate. That is a later optimisation,
  not M1 work.
